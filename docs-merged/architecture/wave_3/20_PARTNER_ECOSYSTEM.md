# Rilavo Product — Partner Ecosystem (E-20)

**Tree:** Product
**Wave:** 3 — Commercial Core
**Status:** Decided
**Depends on:** E-18
**Closes when:** Tested at the first real partner conversation — categories below are a working taxonomy, not a signed roster.

## Partner categories, concrete rather than generic

**Agent-framework vendors** — a natural issuer role. A framework that lets developers build agents could integrate Rilavo issuance as a built-in setup step, so any agent built on that framework gets authorization "for free" from the developer's perspective — directly strengthening Loop D (E-19) without Rilavo needing to reach each individual developer separately.

**API gateway vendors** — a natural verifier role. A gateway product that already sits in front of customers' APIs could offer Rilavo verification as a plugin or built-in option, making adoption a configuration choice rather than a custom integration for every customer behind that gateway.

**Cloud marketplaces** — a distribution surface, not a technical role. Listing the SDK where developers already discover infrastructure tools, lowering the friction of the very first outreach step described in E-18.

## Integrate-not-compete, and why it's stated as a deliberate choice rather than an accident

Nothing structurally stops any of these partners from becoming operators themselves (per the Boundary Map — the protocol is open to anyone). Product's position is to be the best operator, not the only permitted one, which makes "integrate" a genuinely available choice for a partner rather than a euphemism for "use us because you have no alternative."

## Two different partner tracks, with different terms

**Integration partners** — technical integration only, no revenue share, mutual promotion where it's genuinely earned (a real, working integration worth pointing to). Low commitment on both sides, appropriate for the earliest partnerships.

**Certification / reseller partners** — a more formal track, involving revenue share and a defined certification standard, realistically only viable once compliance products (E-10) exist and there's something concrete for a partner to certify against. Not a v0 track.

## Worked example — what stops a partner from just becoming a competitor instead

An agent-framework vendor could, instead of integrating, build its own competing authorization scheme. Nothing in this document prevents that, by design — the actual defense is the same one named in E-26: a competing scheme starts with zero installed verifiers, while integrating with Rilavo gives the framework's users immediate access to whatever verifier base already exists. Integration is the easier, faster path for the partner, not a path Rilavo has to defend through exclusivity.

## What would change this decision

The first real conversation with each category of partner — which will reveal whether the "integrate is just easier" argument above actually lands, or whether a partner needs a stronger incentive (e.g., revenue share, even at the integration-partner tier) than currently planned.

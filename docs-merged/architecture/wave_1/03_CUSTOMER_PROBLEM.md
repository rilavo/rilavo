# Rilavo Product — Customer Problem (E-03)

**Tree:** Product
**Wave:** 1 — System Foundation
**Status:** Decided
**Depends on:** P-01
**Closes when:** Confirmed by the first paying customer — the same underlying evidence as P-03, read here as a sales trigger rather than a design trigger.

## The problem, read commercially

A platform or API provider is, right now, either blocking all inbound AI-agent traffic — losing legitimate business it could otherwise accept — or accepting it blind — carrying liability it has no way to price. Neither is a stable position, and both are already costing the organization something measurable: lost revenue in the first case, unpriced risk in the second.

## Who is the first paying customer

A platform or API provider currently living in one of those two states, with agent traffic already hitting its systems today — not a company anticipating agent traffic someday.

## Economic buyer vs. technical buyer — deliberately distinguished, not collapsed into one persona

The **economic buyer** is whoever owns platform-risk or trust-and-safety budget — the person who can approve spend against a fraud-prevention or compliance line. The **technical buyer** is the engineering team that actually integrates the SDK and lives with the day-to-day developer experience. These are frequently different people with different concerns, and the sales motion (E-22) has to speak to both, not assume satisfying one satisfies the other.

## Why this problem, and not a broader one

A broader pitch — "trust infrastructure for the AI era" — is unfalsifiable and unsellable in a first conversation. "Your platform is currently blocking or blindly accepting AI-agent traffic, and here's what that's costing you" is a specific, checkable claim a prospect can confirm or deny about their own systems in the first five minutes of a call. The narrower claim is the one that actually closes deals; the broader one is the one that sounds better in a pitch deck and converts worse in practice.

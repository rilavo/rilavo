# Rilavo Product — Company Identity (E-02)

**Tree:** Product
**Wave:** 1 — System Foundation
**Status:** Decided
**Depends on:** E-01
**Closes when:** Revisited only on a genuine repositioning, not on a schedule.

## What kind of company this is

Trust infrastructure — closer in kind to security infrastructure than to a KYC vendor or an "AI company." That distinction matters for hiring, positioning, and which comparisons are useful: the right mental model is closer to a certificate authority or a fraud-detection platform than to an identity-verification service, even though the underlying technology touches both.

## First commercial product

Hosted agent-authorization issuance and verification, for developers who don't want to run their own issuer. Not a platform, not a suite, not a marketplace — one narrow, well-scoped product, matching the wedge discipline set in P-03.

## What Rilavo Product should become known for, first

Being correctly scoped and boring, in the good sense — the layer that just works, that a security-conscious engineer trusts precisely because it doesn't try to do more than it says.

## What it must never become known for — a hard line, not a style preference

A data broker. Or a mandatory arbiter of who counts as a "real human" or a "legitimate agent" — either reputation would violate the ethics boundary set in P-40 and would represent an existential risk to the entire company, not a growth strategy to be weighed against upside. This line is stated here, at the identity level, because it needs to constrain every product decision made downstream in this tree, not just the ones that obviously touch it.

## Relationship to the protocol it operates

Rilavo Product runs the reference implementation of an open protocol it does not own. See the System Boundary Map and E-39 for the precise mechanics of that relationship — this document only states the identity implication: the company's reputation is built on being the best operator of something open, not on controlling something closed.

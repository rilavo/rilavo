# Rilavo Product — Competitive Strategy (E-25)

**Tree:** Product
**Wave:** 4 — Survival & Defensibility
**Status:** **Open, by design.** This document builds the most rigorous possible test of the underlying question. It does not answer it — no document can.
**Depends on:** E-06, E-26
**Closes when:** Rilavo survives a real, well-funded competitor. Not before, and not by more analysis in the meantime.

## Three distinct competitor archetypes, not one generic threat

Treating "a well-funded competitor" as a single scenario hides that the right response differs by which of these actually happens:

**1. Hyperscaler-as-feature.** A cloud or platform provider bundles equivalent agent-authorization as a free feature of something customers already use. Not trying to win on product merit — trying to commoditize the whole category. Genuinely dangerous in a different way than the others: it doesn't need to be *better*, only free and already-integrated.

**2. Well-funded, dedicated startup competitor.** Raises capital specifically to compete head-on, tries to out-execute on product, sales, and price. The most conventional competitive threat, and the one E-38's playbook is most directly built around.

**3. Community or open-source fork with no company behind it.** A meaningfully different case, not automatically hostile — per Protocol Principle 5, a fork that strengthens the shared open standard is a success for the ecosystem even where it's a loss for Rilavo Product specifically. This archetype needs to be *evaluated*, not reflexively opposed.

## Early warning signals worth tracking now, before any of these happen

For (1): a major platform's public roadmap or job postings referencing agent-authorization or credential verification as a planned feature. For (2): a competing spec or product announcement explicitly targeting the same wedge (P-03). For (3): fork activity on the reference implementation's public repository, and whether it's compatible or deliberately incompatible with the existing format.

## What's genuinely knowable now, versus what isn't

**Knowable now:** which archetype, if any, is showing early signals; what the response principle is for each (E-38); what assets are being built in the meantime that a competitor of any archetype would have to replicate from zero (E-26).

**Not knowable now, and this document doesn't pretend otherwise:** whether those assets will actually be enough. That depends on how fast Rilavo builds them relative to how fast a real competitor moves — a race that hasn't started yet because v0 hasn't launched.

## The single honest test this document sets

Not "does Rilavo have a competitive strategy" — it does, above. The real test is: **when a real competitor of any of these three archetypes appears, does Rilavo's response come from this document, prepared in advance, or does it get improvised under pressure.** That's a test this document can pass or fail immediately, regardless of whether the competitive outcome itself is known for years.

## What would change this document

Nothing resolves the underlying competitive question by editing this file further. What would genuinely change it: a real competitor of one of these three archetypes actually appearing — at which point this document either guided a prepared response or it didn't, and that outcome itself becomes the evidence for E-26's moat scorecard.

# Rilavo Product — Product Moat (E-26)

**Tree:** Product
**Wave:** 4 — Survival & Defensibility
**Status:** **Open, by design.** What follows is a scorecard for measuring the moat once real operation exists — not a claim that the moat currently exists.
**Depends on:** E-09, E-25
**Closes when:** Accumulates through real operation. It does not get decided on paper.

## Why "the moat is real" has to mean something measurable, not just asserted

Four components were named across this project as the moat: issuer reputation, fraud-intelligence depth, standards legitimacy, and integration density. Naming them isn't the same as having them. This document's job is to define, for each, a specific proxy that could actually be checked at a given point in time — turning "we have a moat" from a claim into a test.

## The scorecard

| Component | Measurable proxy | Earliest it's checkable |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer reputation | Rolling 12-month uptime percentage, incident count, incident severity (per P-23's classification) | Month 12 |
| Fraud-intelligence depth | Number of distinct pattern-signals cleared through the five-account threshold (E-09); measured reduction in false-block rate across the customer base | Incrementally from month 6 (E-21) |
| Standards legitimacy | Invitations to relevant standards-body working groups; citations in third-party interoperability notes (P-18) | 12–24 months, slower-moving by nature |
| Integration density | Number of live, distinct agent-framework and gateway partnerships (E-20) | Incrementally, from first partner integration |

## The honest state of this scorecard today

**Every cell in the "checkable" column is in the future.** v0 hasn't launched. There is no uptime history, no fraud-signal count, no standards-body relationship, no partner integration yet. This document builds the ruler. It does not yet have anything to measure with it, and marking any of the four components as currently "strong" would be exactly the fabricated confidence this entire project has been built to avoid.

## What would falsify the moat, not just confirm it

This scorecard has to be able to say "the moat isn't holding," not only "the moat is holding." A falsification reading looks like: uptime and incident history no better than a fast-following competitor's; false-block rate not meaningfully improving with observed volume; no standards-body traction after a reasonable window; partner integrations stalling. Any of these, sustained, would be real evidence the moat isn't compounding the way the rest of this project has assumed it would — and this document commits to reading that evidence as such, not explaining it away.

## Why this is a different kind of document than E-06 through E-21

Those documents describe decisions Rilavo makes. This one describes a test the world administers. No amount of additional writing moves any cell in the scorecard — only time and real operating data do.

## What would change this document

The first 6–12 months of real data against each proxy above. This document's job, until then, is only to make sure that data gets collected against a framework decided in advance, rather than a story assembled afterward to fit whatever happens.

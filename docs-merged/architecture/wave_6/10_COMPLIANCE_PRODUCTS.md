# Rilavo Product — Compliance Products (E-10)

**Tree:** Product
**Wave:** 6 — Future Horizons
**Status:** Decided (infrastructure, not certification — the hard line below); Open (first market, gated on P-35/P-36)
**Depends on:** P-35, P-36
**Closes when:** The day either P-35 or P-36 actually ships — not before, since there's no compliance product to sell against a claim class that doesn't exist yet.

## The hard line, stated so it can never get blurred in a sales conversation

**Rilavo produces a verifiable audit trail. Rilavo does not certify that a customer's overall compliance program satisfies any regulator.** These are different claims, and conflating them — even implicitly, even in marketing language rather than a contract — would misrepresent what infrastructure can actually attest to versus what a customer's own legal and compliance function has to determine for itself.

## Worked example — the question this line exists to answer honestly

A prospective customer asks: "if we use Rilavo, are we EU AI Act compliant?" **The honest answer is no.** Using Rilavo's content-provenance infrastructure (P-36), once it exists, produces the specific technical artifact Article 50 requires — machine-detectable, interoperable marking of synthetic content — which is a real, necessary contribution to that customer's compliance posture. It is not, by itself, a certification that the customer's broader program (disclosure practices, downstream handling, everything outside what Rilavo technically touches) satisfies the regulation as a whole. A sales conversation that blurs this distinction to close a deal faster creates exactly the liability exposure E-28 already flags as a reason this entire area needs real counsel before launch.

## The real, dated, external demand this product category responds to — not manufactured

The EU AI Act's Article 50 imposes fines reaching €15 million or 3% of global turnover for failing to mark synthetic media appropriately, with its Code of Practice pushing toward the exact kind of interoperable, cross-provider verification this protocol is built to provide. Separately, Nigeria's 2026 identity and data-protection regime — CBN's tiered customer-due-diligence rules, the NIMC Act, and a Data Protection Act carrying criminal penalties for mishandled identity records — makes "prove eligibility without transmitting the raw identifier" a genuinely valuable, budget-line compliance product in that specific jurisdiction, not just a nice architectural property.

## What is genuinely not decided here

Which market opens first. This is explicitly named in P-35 as an Product decision requiring real market evaluation — this document doesn't make that call, and building a specific jurisdictional product packaging before that call is made would be designing ahead of a decision that isn't this document's to make.

## What would change this decision

Either P-35 or P-36 shipping for real — at which point this document graduates from principle and regulatory context into an actual product specification, informed by whichever claim class arrived first and whichever market Product has, by then, actually chosen.
